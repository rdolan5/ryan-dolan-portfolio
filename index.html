Project 01

IRIS: a balloon payload that flies itself home

Org
IrishSat, Notre Dame
Dates
Aug 2025 – present
My role
Structures: design, test, validation, fabrication
Also
Power wiring

IRIS is a 5.53 lb box that rides a weather balloon to about 89,000 ft, lets go, drops on a parachute, then switches to a steerable parafoil and flies to a landing spot the team picks. I own structures design, testing, validation and fabrication on it, from the internal shelving to the waterjet-cut chassis, plus the power wiring.

  • 89,294 ftflown
  • 30+drop tests from 300 ft
  • 30%+less drag uncertainty from CFD
  • 2.5+ hheld at −10 °C

The brief

  • Land the payload where we pick, so it can fly again.
  • 5.53 lb inside 13.5 × 8 × 7.1 in, ~$1,200 a unit.
  • Survive 70 min of climb, air far below freezing, and two deployment snaps.
  • Rebuild between flights without a teardown. Parts made on campus.

Tools

  • SolidWorks
  • Ansys FEA
  • Ansys CFD
  • 6061-T6
  • Carbon plate
  • Printed shelving
  • Waterjet
  • CNC mill
  • Cold chamber testing

What I did

  • Designed the internal shelving, then rebuilt it. Shelves lift out as modules, so a servo, camera or flight computer swaps in minutes, not a teardown. The current version adds a bottom and a middle shelf that route power and signal separately, which killed the wire tangling and keeps battery mass low for a steadier hang.
  • Ran CFD on the deployment mechanism, cut drag uncertainty 30%+, then sized it in Ansys.
  • Machined the 6061 chassis plates on the waterjet.
  • Wired 3 isolated circuits, then ran the cold, structural and electrical test plan. The box held 2.5+ h at −10 °C.
  • Flown to 89,294 ft, plus 30+ drop tests from 300 ft.

Project 03

Rube Goldberg machine

Course
AME 21267 Design Tools I
Dates
Spring 2026
My role
DFM / DFA owner, 9 of 32 parts
Also
Material sourcing

A machine that shuts off a phone alarm. One push starts it, gravity does the rest, and a stylus taps the screen at the end. 32 parts in 6 subassemblies, 30 released drawings, and it came in under budget. It sets up in about 15 seconds against a 30-second target and shut the alarm off every run.

  • 15 ssetup vs. 30 s target
  • 32parts, all machinable
  • 30released drawings
  • Every runalarm shut off

The brief

  • Do one simple thing in as many steps as possible: 8 in a row, under 4 min.
  • Every part machinable. No wood, no glue, no printed parts. That rule drove the build.
  • Set up in 30 s or less, inside a 24 × 36 in footprint, under a $300 cap.
  • Gravity only after the first push.

Tools

  • SolidWorks
  • DFM / DFA
  • ASME Y14.5
  • GD&T
  • 6061 plate, bar, rod
  • Bandsaw
  • Drill press
  • Hand tapping
  • BOMs & sourcing

What I did

  • Owned design for assembly and manufacturability. I reviewed every part against the tools we had before release, and drove the five changes that came out of it.
  • Designed the slide and the domino platform, 9 of the 32 parts, then cut, drilled, tapped and filed them myself.
  • Cut setup to about 15 seconds, half the target, by fixing how the subassemblies locate against each other.
  • Sourced the material for the whole machine and brought it in under budget.
Drawn in CAD → built in the shop. The five calls I made once the tools were the constraint.
PartChange and why
Pendulum6 pieces → 3. Extra pieces did nothing.
SlideCompound angles → simple cuts, screwed together.
Domino platformAluminum → foam board with aluminum stiffeners and rails. Too heavy, then it spun on its rod.
Seesaw leverAcrylic → aluminum. Acrylic could not push the next step.
Lever baseTriangle → square with foam feet. Small angled cuts are slow to clamp.

Project 04

Vibration isolation for a satellite on Falcon 9

Type
Individual course project
Dates
Aug – Dec 2025
Tools
MATLAB, Python
Inputs
Falcon 9 User's Guide, Moog SoftRide

A rocket shakes hard on the way up and the satellite bolted to it feels all of that. I took the shake input straight from SpaceX's published Falcon 9 User's Guide and sized rubber isolators for a 2,982 kg satellite from 5 to 100 Hz, after validating the model three ways in MATLAB and Python.

  • 0.12%sine case vs. hand solution
  • 1.131 gworst case, winning setup
  • 68.85 Hzisolated frequency
  • 5–9×load swing from damping alone

The brief

  • Pick the isolator and count that leave the satellite shaken as little as possible from 5 to 100 Hz.
  • Keep the isolated frequency above 25 Hz, with 4 isolators or more.
  • Shake input from SpaceX's Falcon 9 User's Guide, 0.5 to 0.9 g.
  • Stiffness from Moog's SoftRide datasheet: 1,000 / 3,000 / 6,500 lb/in.

Tools

  • Base-excited SDOF
  • MATLAB ode45
  • Python
  • NumPy
  • SciPy
  • Model validation
  • Sensitivity study

What I did

  • Validated the model three ways first. Steady push settles at −6.00 mm and 180 N, sine shake lands within 0.12% of the hand solution, backwards check to 0.37 mm.
  • Swept every legal combination: UniFlex-1000, 1.131 g worst case, 68.85 Hz isolated. The 3,187 isolators it wants are what the 25 Hz floor forces under 2,982 kg, and I flagged that rather than ship the number.
  • Showed damping drives the answer. The project said 0.40, the datasheet says 3–5%, and the load moves from 1.13 g to 6–10 g on that one assumption.
